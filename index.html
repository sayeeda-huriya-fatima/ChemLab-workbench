Electrolytic Mobility Theory:

Initial conductance is high due to fast H⁺ (λ° = 349.8 S·cm²/mol). As NaOH is added, H⁺ is replaced by slower Na⁺ (λ° = 50.1), causing linear drop. Past neutralization (V₂), excess OH⁻ (λ° = 198.6) causes sharp linear rise.

Analytical Intersection & Algebraic Derivation

Exact Proof

Equivalence volume V₂ is solved analytically at the intersection of the two least-squares regression lines:

• Arm 1 (Descending): y = m₁x + c₁
• Arm 2 (Ascending): y = m₂x + c₂
x = (c₂ - c₁) / (m₁ - m₂) = V₂ = 10.00 mL
Residual Analysis: Goodness of fit R₁² = 0.998, R₂² = 0.997 (High experimental concordance).

Calculation Steps (Syllabus Formula)

1. Normality of Hydrochloric Acid:
N₁ × V₁ = N₂ × V₂  ⟹  N₁ = (N₂ × V₂) / V₁
N₁ = (0.10 N × 10.00 mL) / 40.0 mL = 0.0250 N
2. Strength of Hydrochloric Acid Solution:
Strength (g/L) = Normality (N₁) × Equivalent Weight of HCl (36.5 g/equiv)
Strength = 0.0250 N × 36.5 = 0.9125 g/L
